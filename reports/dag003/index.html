DAG0036 min read

The scope is a short basket of broad fund-like initial 13D subjects with inversions removed, entered at quarter ends, over seven exposed windows. Guess: The DAG002 shortfall of fund-like initial 13D subjects is a real loss, not a benchmark artifact, and it can be captured by a short that pays over the months after the filing.

Simplified

We tested whether the stocks that lost money after a fund's initial 13D could be profitably shorted. The loss is real: these stocks land in the worst 5% of similar stocks almost twice as often as chance. But they were already lagging before the filing and keep lagging for a year, so the 13D marks a weak kind of company rather than an event that fades. The tradable liquid short earned +2.30% a quarter after borrow costs, but it was too tied to the market under our frozen rule. The bigger +4.49% sits in small stocks that may be costly or impossible to borrow. This closes the idea for month-scale alpha.

Reading of the evidence

The packet supports relative underperformance in an exposed sample; it does not establish a capturable outright short profit. The specification fails its frozen advancement rule.

Verified from [study results]([local path]):

  • C0’s 233 observations have a mean within-cell percentile of 44.81, below 50 in 6/7 windows, with reported permutation p = 0.0036. Its 21 bottom-tail hits (9.01%) suggest more than merely missing extreme winners.
  • N0 is near neutral: percentile 49.88, p = 0.488, across 43 observations. This satisfies S3, though nonsignificance in a small control does not establish absence of benchmark bias.
  • L0’s adverse-borrow spread averages +2.30% per quarter, positive in 5/7 windows; doubling transaction costs leaves +1.80%. These are benchmark-relative spreads. They do not show that the shorted stocks fell outright.
  • S5 fails: slope −0.251474, outside the registered [−0.25, 0.25] range. The miss is economically small, but the [study plan]([local path]) requires stopping if any S1–S5 gate fails.
  • S6 passes narrowly: Q+1 percentile shortfall 5.35 points, versus 4.66 averaged across Q+3/Q+4. Q+4’s percentile is still 44.14. I infer only weak evidence of decay, not a clearly temporary effect.

These checks establish consistency between the supplied tables and decision rule, not independent verification of the estimates.

  • Selection and mechanism: Pre-filing percentile is already 44.26, p = 0.0176, below 50 in all five available windows.
