DAG004: cured late filers

DAG0049 min read

Status: dead. Expert, 2026-11-04. The mechanism failed, not just a specification. Cured late filers do not recover after the cure; they keep lagging, on exposed 2024-2026 data and on unseen 2016-2024 data. The shortfall was already present before the notice (45.4 percentile, against 45.3 after the cure), so it is a trait of chronically weak names, not an event effect. Scope: NT 10-K and NT 10-Q notices cured within 92 days, quarter-end entry, cells matched on dollar volume and prior return. Month-scale event alpha is ruled out at this scope. A persistent short of thin late filers is outside this line and undetermined, because borrow data is missing.

Guess: A late-filing notice (NT 10-K or NT 10-Q) drives mandate- and risk-limited holders out; once the late report is filed the uncertainty is resolved, but the discount clears only over the following months as holders and attention return, so cured late filers earn excess return that fades over months.

Simplified

We tested whether companies that file a late-filing notice and then file the late report recover over the next months. They do not: they keep falling behind similar stocks, by about 12% a quarter in 2024-2026 and about 4-6% a quarter in 2016-2024. Shorting them looked profitable on paper, but these companies were already lagging before the notice, so the notice marks weak companies rather than a shock that wears off. The large, easy-to-borrow names showed no profit, and most of the gain sits in small stocks that are often expensive or impossible to borrow. This closes the idea for month-scale alpha.

  • DA009: Cured filers averaged −11.91% quarterly net matched excess, with no positive window out of seven and a pooled percentile of 40.39. Three of the four declared advancement gates failed. Validity

  • Leakage and timing: The written rules use notices, cures and financial submissions available before formation. That is sensible. DA011 also explicitly applies formation-universe conditioning to both sides of the pre-notice comparison, addressing DA010’s documented asymmetry. Implementation cannot be checked.
  • Selection and inference: Matching dollar volume and prior returns does not establish the proposed holder-exit mechanism. The near-equal pre- and post-notice weakness is direct contrary evidence. There are 30 time windows, not 803 independent observations; reported window clustering and Newey–West treatment are useful, while S2’s conventional regression t does not establish robustness to serial dependence.
  • Split repairs: Correction 003 converts near-round large price moves into implied splits and treats other unexplained moves as real. This can erase genuine crashes and rallies, affecting events and controls differently; it is not uniformly conservative. It also changes how the original 8.8% flagged-name problem is resolved against the 1% stop rule. Missing audits prevent verification of the resulting error rate. ([Correction 003]([local path]))
  • Other data assumptions: Flat unexplained exits and zero-valued unpriceable spin-offs remain material approximations; results count 206 unpriced spin-offs without exposure attribution. Reported broad-basket signs survive former-SPAC exclusion, own-prefix restriction and adverse control-exit treatment, which is reassuring but does not validate individual marks.
  • Costs and execution: DA011 includes declared transaction costs and total-return corporate-action treatment. Nevertheless, liquidity/capitalization thresholds do not demonstrate borrow availability. At 20% annual borrow the broad spread is approximately zero, and the preferred tier already fails at 3%. Close-based spreads lack locates, recalls, margin, financing and an executable account path. ([Plan]([local path]), [results]([local path]))
